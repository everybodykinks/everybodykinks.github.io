01 / About

A separate space for the teaching side of kink.

Okay, so this is my education page. Same me you know from my main, just a separate space for the teaching and workshop side of things.

I've been doing this for 18 years now, which is a genuinely unsettling number to type out, and most of that has been spent presenting and teaching, not just playing.

Somewhere in there I got tired of watching the same handful of TikTok and YouTube educators teach kink on the same body, over and over, like the rest of us don't exist. Standard build, standard mobility, standard nervous system, standard everything — and usually straight, cis, and monogamous while they're at it.

Meanwhile, I'm sitting here thinking about how impact play actually changes when someone's got chronic pain, or how a negotiation conversation needs to work differently when someone's autistic or living with PTSD, or what a scene looks like when it's not built around a straight cis pairing by default.

Nobody was covering that. So I decided to.

02 / The approach

Adapted, not generalized.

Good education starts with the person in front of you — not an imaginary default student.

01

Consent is the floor

Consent, communication, boundaries, check-ins, and aftercare are foundational skills, not advanced topics you graduate from.

02

Bodies are different

Mobility, pain, anatomy, sensory needs, energy, and other physical realities can change how a technique or scene needs to be approached.

03

Brains are different

Communication and negotiation can look different for autistic people, people living with trauma, and people with different sensory or processing needs.

04

Relationships are different

Kink doesn't have to fit a straight, cis, monogamous template. Queer, polyamorous, relationship-anarchist, and other structures belong in the center of the conversation.

01

02

03

Neurodivergence

Communication, sensory considerations, processing time, and negotiation without assuming one brain.

04

Trauma-aware practice

Thinking carefully about triggers, agency, communication, and support without treating people as fragile.

05

LGBTQIA+ kink

Education that doesn't quietly make heterosexual, cisgender experience the default.

06

Polyamory & kink

Negotiating dynamics across multiple partners, metamours, agreements, and changing relationship structures.

07

Impact & sensation

Understanding how bodies, communication, context, and adaptation shape a scene.

08

Community & teaching

Creating spaces where newer people can learn without being talked down to — or left behind.
